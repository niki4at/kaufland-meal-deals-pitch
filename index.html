The opportunity

We already own all three components. They are just never sold together.

Food-to-go is the fastest-monetising grocery mission in Europe, and it grows through bundles, not footfall.

Asset 1

ToGo counter (grab-and-go)

100+ items

Ready and semi-ready meals positioned at the store entrance in every remodelled store. Sandwiches, wraps, salads, hot snacks. Today it sells single items at full price with no attach mechanics.

Asset 2

In-store bakery

60%+ margin

Fresh bake-off across the estate, the highest-margin fresh category in the store. Banitsa, croissants, pretzels and desserts are natural lunch companions with near-zero marginal placement cost.

Asset 3

Drinks + XTRA

1M MAU

Chilled drinks with strong supplier funding appetite, plus Kaufland Card XTRA: 2M+ registered users, 1M+ monthly active, and an AI engine already pushing 10 personalised coupons weekly.

The gap: no fixed lunch price point, no cross-zone bundle, no habit loop. A customer buying a €2.99 wrap today leaves €2+ of attachable spend on the table on every visit.
The benchmark: UK food-to-go hits £24.9bn in 2026 (+3.4%); spend per visit is up +8.5% YoY, driven by structured bundles and add-ons rather than traffic (Lumina Intelligence).
ToGo concept: Kaufland BG store communications, 100+ ready/semi-ready items at entrance zone. Loyalty: Kaufland Card XTRA, 2M+ registered, 1M+ monthly active (company statements, May 2026). UK FTG: Lumina Intelligence 2026 forecast.

Evidence base

Meal deals are the most proven bundle mechanic in European grocery

UK lunch meal deal price ladder (GBP, 2025)

A 20-year-old mechanic still growing; Tesco repriced twice since 2022 with no volume loss reported

Volume proof

Tesco Ireland sells 70,000+ meal deals weekly across 194 stores through one supplier contract alone (Freshways, €15M/yr). Tesco UK's Clubcard data ranks the meal deal among its highest-frequency purchases; ~80% of shoppers transact identified.

Behavioural mechanics that transfer to Bulgaria

1 · Attach: a fixed trio price converts single-item buyers into 3-item baskets.
2 · Habit: one price, zero decision cost, daily repeatability; lunch is a 5x/week mission.
3 · Identification: a card-only price gap moves lunch traffic onto XTRA, feeding the AI coupon engine with weekday behaviour we currently never observe.

Competitive window

No Bulgarian grocer runs a structured cross-category meal deal today. Lidl's format has no service counters or hot zones to copy it at parity. First mover sets the reference price.

Tesco pricing: BBC, Aug 2025. Ireland volumes: Business Plus, Freshways contract 2025. Clubcard penetration ~80%: BBC. UK FTG market: Lumina Intelligence 2026. Competitive scan: BG chains' current promotional programmes, Jan–Aug 2026.

Cannibalisation is modelled, not ignored

*We assume 45% of deal buyers would have bought something anyway (avg €2.90 at 30% margin). Their existing GP is netted out. Even so, the deal upgrades a €2.90 single-item basket into a €4.19 trio, so the mechanic is accretive per transaction in every scenario.

Second-order effects

The deal is also a traffic, data and margin-mix instrument

Frequency: the real prize

Lunch is a 5x/week mission; a weekly big shop is 1x. If the deal converts 3% of the 1M+ active XTRA users into +2 extra store visits per month, and each extra visit carries just €2 of non-deal spend, that is ~€1.4M/year of additional halo revenue on top of the deal itself.

Data: weekday behaviour we never see

The €0.50 card gap pushes lunch transactions onto XTRA. Every identified deal feeds the existing AI coupon engine with a new behavioural dimension (weekday, time-of-day, meal preference), improving targeting of the 10 weekly personalised coupons already in production.

Waste: bundles clear fresh stock

The ToGo counter and bakery carry the store's highest write-off risk. A predictable daily deal volume smooths production planning and can absorb short-dated items into the mains group, cutting fresh shrink by a targeted 1–2 p.p. in the two categories.

Halo model: base case, steady state (€M / year)

Deal revenue + frequency halo + waste saving, net of cannibalisation
Halo math: 1M MAU × 3% × 24 extra visits/yr × €2 ≈ €1.44M. Waste: BG fresh shrink benchmarks 3–6% of category sales; 1.5 p.p. on ~€25M ToGo+bakery pilot-relevant sales ≈ €0.4M. Both tracked as pilot KPIs before being claimed.

Systems integration

It is a promotion, not a product: standard SAP, zero new SKUs

The deal maps 1:1 onto the Mix-and-Match / Bonus Buy pattern that the Schwarz SAP retail stack supports out of standard. Components keep their EANs, inventory and category P&L.

SAP S/4HANA RetailPromotion definition

Three component groups (Mains / Bakery / Drinks) as article hierarchies. Bonus Buy: "1 of each group → €4.49"; card-linked condition €3.99. Owned by Purchasing, per store cluster.

Kaufland App · XTRALoyalty layer

Card price applied via existing loyalty pricing; AI coupon engine targets lapsed lunchers and upsells the Premium tier. Deal page and combo inspiration in the app.

Why zero new SKUs matters

No new article master data, no new supply chain lanes, no double inventory. Range changes = editing a group list, not relaunching a product.

Clean margin accounting

Pro-rata discount allocation in the transaction log means each category (Fresh, Bakery, Beverages) reports true post-deal margin. No P&L disputes between category managers.

Fits the Schwarz IT roadmap

Schwarz IT already runs GK POS group-wide and SAP CAR as the retail data hub; omnichannel price consistency for the app comes from the same promotion objects.

Pattern: SAP Retail Bonus Buy / Mix-and-Match with standard offer replication to GK POS ("SAP Store Solutions by GK", SAP's strategic POS partner) and receipt upload to SAP CAR. Schwarz IT / GK relationship: GK Software corporate news; Retail Optimiser.

De-risking

12-week Sofia pilot with hard gates, then a data-driven rollout decision

Weeks 1–8 · Build

Configuration, dashboards, fixtures in 8 Sofia stores (mix of office-dense and residential catchments for honest read-across).

Weeks 9–20 · Live pilot

Daily CAR dashboard; weekly trade review with Purchasing; price and assortment tuning allowed at weeks 12 and 16 only, to keep the test clean.

Week 21 · Gate review

Go / tune / stop decision against the KPI table. Go = national rollout in 2 waves over 8 weeks, ~35 stores per wave.

Month 9 · Full estate

All 71 stores live; premium tier and breakfast window activated; supplier drink-slot tender for the next two quarters.

What the pilot must answer

True attach rate in Bulgarian shopping culture, the right €3.99 vs €4.49 gap, weekday vs weekend skew, and whether office catchments outperform enough to justify differentiated assortment. Every question maps to a dashboard, not an opinion.

Control cohort: matched XTRA users in non-pilot Sofia stores, tracked via loyalty IDs in CAR. Cannibalisation measured as delta in solo ToGo/bakery/drink units among deal buyers vs their own 12-week pre-period.
